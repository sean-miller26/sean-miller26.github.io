Stats & Impact Dashboard

Role: Product Designer
Scope: Profiles, dashboards, certification tools
Timeline: 12 weeks
Outcome: Increased motivation and platform trust, reduced support tickets

Making Invisible Contributions Feel Meaningful

Participants came to the platform because they cared. They showed up, classified data, and helped real research move forward. But when they tried to understand how much they had helped, or whether their work mattered at all, the experience fell apart.

Stats were scattered across the site. Numbers did not always match. Certificates had to be requested manually. Educators and corporate partners depended on staff just to see group impact.

For a platform built on collective effort, impact was hard to see.

This case study explains how we turned fragmented, unreliable numbers into a system that helped participants see themselves as part of something larger, and gave them a reason to continue.

The Tension

Early research exposed a clear gap:

  • Participants cared deeply about the mission.
  • Many could not tell if their individual effort made a difference.

Casual contributors, or dabblers as we called them, slowly stopped participating. Educators emailed us again and again for stats they could not access. Power users kept their own spreadsheets because they did not trust what the platform showed.

The issue was not missing data. It was missing clarity, trust, and motivation.

The Challenge

How might we make volunteer impact visible, trustworthy, and motivating, while also reducing complexity for users and support load for the team?

We were not just designing a page. We were changing how participants understood their work.

What Was Broken

Stats Were Siloed

Numbers lived in different places and were calculated in different ways. Viewing your stats was clunky. Comparing them was harder.

Motivation Leveled Off

There were no clear markers of progress. For many people, volunteering felt endless and thankless, especially at the beginning.

Autonomy Was Missing

Educators and corporate partners had to request certificates and stats by hand. This slowed them down and created bottlenecks for our team.

Design Principles

To stay aligned across teams and features, we grounded the work in four principles:

Modular

Components flexible enough to work across profiles, groups, and classrooms.

Streamlined

Information presented clearly, without overload.

Trusted

Stats accurate, transparent, and consistent everywhere.

Autonomous

Users able to access, generate, and share their own stats without staff involvement.

These principles shaped every decision.

We also set a clear boundary. We would not gamify the system. Zooniverse supports real research. Careful, thoughtful classifications matter more than speed or volume. Points, ranks, or badges would push the wrong behavior.

Instead, we focused on visibility. Participants needed to understand the value of their work without turning science into a competition.

Research: Listening Before Designing

I spoke with educators, experienced contributors, and first-time participants to understand where motivation held and where it faded.

I heard:

  • "I know I am helping, but I do not know how much."
  • "I need something concrete to show my students."
  • "I stopped checking stats because they did not make sense."

At the same time, I reviewed stats systems on other platforms and audited our existing React components. I looked for patterns that reduced mental effort while still feeling meaningful.

Key Insights

  • Autonomy supports retention: Participants wanted instant access to stats and volunteer certificates without waiting for approval.
  • Shared visibility builds engagement: Group and classroom stats encouraged pride and friendly comparison.

Stats were not just numbers. They carried emotional weight.

The Solution: Turning Data Into a Story

We designed a unified Stats and Impact Dashboard that treated volunteering as an ongoing effort, not a single action.

A Single Source of Truth

All stats lived in one place. Calculations were consistent and clearly explained. This rebuilt trust with skeptical users.

Clear Connections and Trends

Participants could see how their totals broke down by project. They could compare their activity with others in a group. They could discover which projects their peers were spending time on.

Built-In Validation

I proposed volunteer certification directly within the stats page. Certificates could be generated instantly, without contacting support. They felt official and tangible.

Impact at Every Level

  • Individuals tracked their own progress.
  • Educators saw classroom activity in one view.
  • Organizations measured collective contribution.

The same underlying system supported each case. One well-designed component could be reused across the site, which reduced development effort and improved consistency.

Testing: From Reactive to Intentional

Before this work, testing was mostly reactive. We addressed issues when something broke.

I introduced a structured beta testing process. We built a list of both power users and new participants. We tested early and often. We checked usability, validated calculations across edge cases, and confirmed certificate generation worked from start to finish.

This gave the team confidence in both the interface and the data behind it.

Launch & Impact

When the new stats page, certificate generator, and group dashboards launched, the response was clear.

Blog posts received over 12,000 views. Thousands of groups were created in the first month.

Participants began bookmarking their dashboards. No one asked them to. They chose to return.

Measurable Results

  • Certification-related support tickets dropped.
  • Group creation increased as educators and organizations tracked shared progress.
  • Group classifications quickly reached the millions, showing that visible impact and light competition kept people engaged longer.

The Outcome

The new stats system displayed data, but it also changed perception.

Participants felt ownership over their numbers. Progress was easier to see. Contribution felt real.

Many returned because they wanted to keep their streak going. For the internal team, built-in certificate generation reduced long-standing support requests.

Classrooms and organizations began marking shared milestones together. Participation turned into recognition.

The system strengthened trust, supported community, and made volunteering feel concrete.

Lessons Learned

  • Align early across teams: Infrastructure, content, and marketing all shaped this work. Early collaboration prevented rework later.
  • Clarity comes first: People need to understand what they are seeing before they can feel inspired by it.
  • Proactive design scales: When users can complete tasks on their own, teams spend less time unblocking them.

This project changed how we communicate value across the platform. It reinforced the need to design for both first-time visitors and long-term contributors.

Impact does not need to be competitive. It needs to be visible.
