Stats and Impact Dashboard

Role: Product Designer
Scope: Profiles, dashboards, certification tools
Timeline: 12 weeks
Outcome: Increased motivation and platform trust, reduced support tickets

Making Invisible Contributions Feel Meaningful

Volunteers came to the platform because they cared. They showed up, classified data, and contributed to real research. But when they tried to understand how much they’d helped, or whether their work mattered at all, the experience broke down. Stats were scattered across the platform. Numbers didn’t always match. Certificates required manual requests. Educators and corporate partners relied on staff just to see group impact. For a platform built on collective effort, impact felt strangely invisible. This case study tells the story of how we transformed fragmented, untrusted numbers into a system that helped volunteers see themselves as part of something bigger—and motivated them to keep going.

The Tension

Early research revealed a contradiction:

  • Volunteers were highly motivated by the mission.
  • But many couldn't tell if their individual effort mattered.

Casual contributors quietly dropped off. Educators emailed for stats they couldn't access. Power users built their own spreadsheets because they didn't trust what the platform showed them.

The problem wasn't a lack of data. It was a lack of clarity, trust, and ownership.

The Challenge

How might we make volunteer impact visible, trustworthy, and motivating—without increasing complexity for users or support load for the team?

We weren't just designing a page. We were redefining the relationship between volunteers and their work.

What Was Broken

Stats Didn't Feel Reliable

Numbers appeared in different places, calculated differently. Users questioned whether they were accurate at all.

Motivation Plateaued

Without progress indicators or milestones, volunteering felt endless and abstract—especially for new users.

Autonomy Was Missing

Educators, classrooms, and corporate partners had to request certificates and stats manually, creating friction on both sides.

Design Principles

To guide decisions across many features and stakeholders, we aligned on four principles:

Modular

Components flexible enough to scale across profiles, groups, and classrooms.

Streamlined

Information surfaced clearly, without overwhelming users.

Trusted

Stats had to be accurate, transparent, and consistent everywhere.

Autonomous

Users could access, generate, and share their own data without intervention.

These principles became our filter for every design choice.

One constraint guided everything we didn't add: the goal was not to gamify the system. Zooniverse supports real research and real science, where thoughtful, high-quality classifications matter far more than speed or volume. Introducing points, ranks, or badges risked encouraging users to game the system rather than engage carefully.

Instead of extrinsic rewards, we focused on meaningful visibility—helping volunteers understand the value of their contributions without turning science into a scoreboard.

Research: Listening Before Designing

We spoke with educators, super-users, and first-time volunteers to understand where motivation sparked—and where it faded.

We heard things like:

  • "I know I'm helping, but I don't know how much."
  • "I need something concrete to show my students."
  • "I stopped checking stats because they didn't make sense."

In parallel, I audited stats systems across other platforms and reviewed existing React components to identify patterns that reduced cognitive load while still feeling rewarding.

Key Insights

  • Autonomy drives retention: Volunteers wanted to generate certificates and access stats instantly.
  • Social proof fuels engagement: Group and classroom stats encouraged friendly competition and collective pride.

Stats weren't just informational—they were emotional.

The Solution: Turning Data Into a Story

We designed a unified Stats and Impact Dashboard that reframed volunteering as a journey, not a transaction.

A Single Source of Truth

All stats now lived in one place, calculated consistently and explained clearly. This alone rebuilt trust.

Progress You Could Feel

Milestones, totals, and visual hierarchy helped volunteers understand where they were—and how far they'd come.

Built-In Celebration

Certificates could be generated instantly, without emailing support. Achievements felt earned, not administrative.

Impact at Every Level

  • Individuals could track personal progress.
  • Educators could view classroom impact.
  • Organizations could measure collective contribution.

Same system. Different lenses. This meant a carefully designed component could be used over several areas of the site, easing dev-effort.

Testing: From Reactive to Intentional

Before this project, testing was informal and reactive.

I introduced a structured beta testing process that allowed us to:

  • Identify usability pain points early
  • Verify data accuracy across edge cases
  • Confirm certificate generation worked end-to-end

This shift gave the team confidence—not just in the design, but in the data behind it.

Launch & Impact

When the new stats page, certificate generator, and group dashboards launched, the response was immediate.

Blog posts alone received 12,000+ views.

More tellingly, volunteers began bookmarking their dashboards—an unprompted signal that the page held real, ongoing value.

Measurable Results

  • Support tickets related to certification dropped significantly.
  • Group creation surged as educators and organizations tracked shared progress.
  • Group classifications reached the millions, showing that visible impact keeps people engaged longer.

The Outcome

The new stats system did more than visualize data.

It reshaped how volunteers saw themselves within the mission.

Numbers became personal. Progress became visible. Contribution became something worth celebrating.

Volunteers returned not because they had to—but because they wanted to.

For the internal team, the redesign reduced support burden and proved a simple truth: when users are trusted with their own data, they thrive.

Classrooms and organizations began celebrating shared milestones, turning participation into recognition.

This system built trust, strengthened community, and made volunteering feel real.

Lessons Learned

  • Cross-functional alignment early matters: This project touched infrastructure, content, and marketing. Close collaboration ensured ideas were feasible and cohesive.
  • Clarity before inspiration: While storytelling motivates, users must first understand the platform before they can feel inspired by it.
  • Designing for ownership scales better than support: Empowered users reduce friction—for themselves and for teams.

This project changed how we think about communicating value across the platform and reinforced the importance of designing for both first-time visitors and long-term contributors.

Impact doesn't have to be loud—but it does have to be visible.
