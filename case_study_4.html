Volumetric Data Viewer

Role: Product Designer
Scope: 3D volumetric data visualization and interaction
Timeline: 6 weeks
Outcome: Enabled work on complex 3D datasets, laid groundwork for future medical projects

Making the Complex Navigable

To understand how the brain produces thought, emotion, and movement, scientists must map the dense networks formed by billions of neurons. These connections live inside massive 3D volumetric datasets—far beyond what traditional 2D tools can represent.

For researchers, this complexity is expected.

For volunteers, it can be overwhelming.

This project asked a fundamental question: How might we let non-experts meaningfully contribute to cutting-edge brain research without diluting scientific rigor?

The Tension

Volumetric brain data is inherently technical. It demands spatial reasoning, precision, and patience.

Zooniverse's strength, however, lies in accessibility—inviting everyday people into serious science.

Bridging those two worlds meant accepting a constraint: we couldn't simplify the task itself, only the experience of learning it.

The Challenge

How might we design a tool that makes deeply technical, 3D data approachable—without flattening its meaning or accuracy?

Volunteers needed to trace individual neuronal pathways with care. Researchers needed confidence that the data would be usable. The interface had to support both.

What Was Broken (or Missing)

Complex Data

3D volumetric scans contain layers of information that can easily overwhelm new users.

Steep Learning Curve

Even small mistakes could be discouraging if the interface didn't support exploration and recovery.

Lack of Context

Without cues or orientation, it was hard for users without neuroscience backgrounds to understand what they were seeing—or why it mattered.

Goals

We set out to design a viewer that could:

  • Enable volunteers to trace neuronal pathways accurately
  • Break massive brain scans into smaller, manageable cubes
  • Feel simple and familiar, even when the task itself was complex
  • Scale to future volumetric projects beyond neuroscience

The interface needed to teach quietly—through interaction, not instruction.

Guiding Principles

Intuitive

Reduce friction for first-time users

Familiar

Borrow patterns from known 3D tools

Future-Proof

Build a flexible foundation for new datasets

Error-Tolerant

Encourage learning without penalty

These principles helped us decide where to be precise—and where to be forgiving.

Research & Design: Translating 3D Space

Our lead engineer identified open-source components as a technical base. From there, I layered in Zooniverse's design language and interaction patterns.

Key design decisions included:

  • Randomized color markings to distinguish paths
  • Collapsible slice views with color-coded axes
  • Clear visual affordances to maintain orientation in 3D space

I drew heavily from familiar 3D design software, prioritizing interactions users might already recognize. Responsive layouts ensured the experience held together across devices.

The goal wasn't to make users experts—it was to help them feel capable.

Testing: Letting Confusion Surface

We began with internal alpha testing to confirm performance and stability across browsers.

Once stable, we moved into beta testing that simulated real volunteer workflows. Importantly, we avoided heavy guidance so we could observe natural behavior.

This surfaced several key insights:

  • Users struggled to annotate multiple marks efficiently
  • The "Add a New Mark" action needed a visible control, not a hidden shortcut

Addressing these issues improved discoverability and reduced hesitation—small changes that significantly increased confidence.

Launch & Impact

Within the first month, volunteers completed 12,000+ classifications.

Each task took between three and ten minutes, with some sessions lasting nearly an hour. Despite the technical challenge, users stayed engaged.

Their work meaningfully reduced researcher effort, accelerating the process of mapping neural pathways and bringing scientists closer to understanding how brain circuits drive behavior.

The Outcome

The Volumetric Viewer marked a turning point for the Zooniverse platform.

For the first time, volunteers could interact with true 3D datasets—unlocking opportunities in neuroscience, pathology, and medical imaging.

What was once a specialized research task became collaborative.

Beyond this project, the viewer laid the groundwork for future CT and MRI-based studies, expanding the reach of citizen science into new scientific and medical domains.

Design acted as a bridge—connecting cutting-edge data with everyday curiosity.

Lessons Learned

This project reinforced the importance of strong cross-functional collaboration when working on technically complex tools. Designing the Volumetric Viewer required close alignment between engineering, research, and design to ensure that scientific requirements, performance constraints, and usability needs were all met.

We learned that introducing advanced data types to a volunteer audience requires clear prioritization: deciding which complexity must remain visible for accuracy, and where the interface can shoulder the burden instead. Early and frequent testing helped us validate those decisions and avoid over-engineering solutions that wouldn't meaningfully improve comprehension.

The project also highlighted the value of designing scalable foundations. By investing in flexible interaction patterns and a robust technical base, we created a viewer that could extend beyond a single neuroscience use case into future CT and MRI-based projects.

Overall, this work strengthened our approach to building advanced scientific tools that are both rigorous and approachable, and it set a clear precedent for how Zooniverse can responsibly expand into more technically demanding domains.
