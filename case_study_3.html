Deeper Engagement Exploration Tool

Role: Product Designer
Scope: Subject pages, tagging, exploration pathways, discussion touchpoints
Timeline: 8 weeks
Outcome: Clearer exploratory pathways, lowered barriers to participation

The Challenge

How might we lower the barrier to deeper exploration while protecting the integrity of real scientific work?

Zooniverse depends on careful classifications. Speed and volume are not the goal. Many advanced participants were already doing more than the interface supported. They opened external databases in other tabs and cross referenced catalogs.

The focus was on enabling curiosity and giving participants the right tools and context so they can explore at their own pace.

Designing for Serendipity Through Context and Exploration

Participants land on a subject page because something caught their eye. A strange galaxy. An odd signal. A pattern they did not expect.

Too often, that curiosity would stop there.

The page showed the image and the conversation. Nothing more. No related subjects. No shared tags. No clear path to explore further.

For a platform built on discovery, the Subject page was not helping people discover much.

This case study covers how I redesigned it into a deeper engagement hub. A place that invites people to investigate connections and contribute with more care.

What Was Broken

Sparse Interface

The Subject page was minimal, but at the cost of context. It did not surface connections that deserved closer study.

Disjointed Conversations

Comments were hard to follow and poorly ordered. Tagging felt outdated and needed modernization.

No Jumping Off Point

There was no smooth way to move between related subjects. Exploration felt cut off.

Goals

I set out to turn the Subject page into a space that supports different levels of engagement:

  • Make tagging and exploration easier for casual users
  • Surface related subjects and patterns to encourage discovery
  • Bring key metadata and external tools into the page for advanced users
  • Reduce friction and lower the barrier to contribution

Whether someone spends thirty seconds or thirty minutes, the experience should feel worthwhile.

Guiding Principles

Engaging

Invite participation without forcing it

Contextual

Discovery needs background and reference points

Exploratory

Make paths clear and easy to follow

Insightful

Help people understand what they are looking at

Research: Learning From Power Users

I led a cross-project workshop with researchers to map shared needs and identify gaps in the current page.

I also interviewed advanced participants who use external tools alongside Zooniverse, including:

  • SIMBAD for object identification
  • VizieR for catalogs and photometry
  • WISEView for infrared imagery

These users were already digging deeper for context.

Research also showed that while some people hesitate to comment, tagging feels more approachable. That made it a strong entry point for broader participation.

The Solution: A Page That Encourages Digging Deeper

I redesigned the Subject page to feel less like a dead end and more like a crossroads.

  • Related subjects create natural paths for exploration
  • Tagging is positioned as a low-friction way to add insight
  • Key external tools are integrated directly into the page

The page does not reward speed. It supports careful exploration.

Testing: Learning Where Expectations Break

Over two months, we tested the redesign with two projects and worked closely with research leads. Every participant had access to a feedback form. We collected qualitative reactions and detailed usability notes.

What We Learned

  • The new tagging system was preferred and lowered the barrier to participation
  • Some interaction patterns did not match expectations shaped by other platforms

That mismatch revealed areas that need refactoring, which I am now guiding with the development team.

We also found issues with commenting: it was not clearly signposted, and as a modal, it blocked the subject itself.

These problems came from late leadership decisions that shifted focus toward discussion. Adding comments here also risks duplicating what the dedicated discussion page already does. That tension needs to be revisited.

Launch: What We Will Be Watching

The redesign will launch soon to the wider Zooniverse Talk pages. After launch, we will monitor:

  • Where users hesitate or drop off
  • Whether tagging feels intuitive without instruction
  • How often people move between Subject pages
  • Whether discussions become more active

This is a significant update. The Talk section has not changed in nearly ten years.

The Outcome

With clearer context and stronger pathways, the Subject page can move beyond a single interaction.

Casual users gain confidence to explore. Advanced users work more efficiently and contribute with better context.

Scientific work stays careful and collaborative.

Lessons Learned

  • Product complexity cannot be assumed: Leadership decisions sometimes conflict with real user behavior.
  • Clear communication is part of the design role: I need to surface workflow risks earlier to avoid major UX disagreements.
  • Feedback grows with exposure: More insight will come once the full experience is live.

Designing for discovery requires restraint. Sometimes the responsible choice is not adding more incentives, but giving curiosity room to unfold.
